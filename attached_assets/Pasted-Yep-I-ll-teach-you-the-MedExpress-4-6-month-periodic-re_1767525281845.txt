## 1) What the “6-month review” actually is

This SOP calls it a **periodic review**. It’s a structured checkpoint to decide whether a patient can **continue GLP-1** under UK product licence expectations.

### The clock starts from:

**The customer’s first GLP-1 order** — **even if it was with another provider**.
If they transfer to you already having had:

* **Mounjaro/Wegovy:** **6+ months** elsewhere → periodic review is requested on their **first order with you**
* **Nevolat (liraglutide):** **4+ months** elsewhere → periodic review is requested on their **first order with you**
  Also: the schedule **resets when a patient switches GLP-1 drug**.

---

## 2) Who gets forced into a periodic review (and when)

### If they have **NOT achieved ≥5% weight loss**:

* **Mounjaro/Wegovy:** after **6 months** use
* **Nevolat:** after **4 months** use

They must complete the periodic review **within a time window**:

* **Mounjaro/Wegovy:** between **month 6 and month 10**
* **Nevolat:** between **month 4 and month 8**

### If they **HAVE achieved ≥5% weight loss**

They’re **not required** to do it — but it remains **optional**:

* **Mounjaro/Wegovy:** optional at **6-monthly intervals**
* **Nevolat:** optional at **4-monthly intervals**

### What happens if they ignore it

They’re **auto-notified via CRM** to complete it in their dashboard.

* **Mounjaro/Wegovy:** they have until **month 10**
* **Nevolat:** they have until **month 8**
  If no response by the deadline:
* **subsequent orders are placed on hold**
* **“incomplete periodic review”** tag is added automatically
* Customer Support follows them up

---

## 3) What the patient actually fills in (so you know what you’re reviewing)

The periodic review questionnaire asks:

1. Are you losing weight at a rate you’re happy with?
2. Side effects in last 2 weeks (some are flagged as **urgent**)
3. New medications / new diagnoses since starting
4. Health benefits noticed (waist, mood, mobility, etc.)
5. Weekly injection schedule adherence
6. Diet changes
7. Activity changes
8. Anything else that may impact weight loss

**Important SOP point:**
✅ **Do NOT request a photo** for periodic review. Photos are required at initial order, **not** for continuing at periodic review.

---

## 4) What YOU do when it lands on your desk (the real workflow)

### Step A — Triage: is it urgent?

If the patient selected an **urgent side effect** (⚠️ flagged options), the periodic review appears in the **Urgent queue**.

**Action:**

* Must be reviewed by a **Senior Prescriber within 8 working hours**
* The customer may need urgent medical input

If urgent:

1. **Telephone contact within 8 working hours**
2. Add tag **Pending Customer Response** if you can’t reach
3. If you can’t reach, **email** them
4. Submit a **Patient Safety Incident**

   * Category: **Clinically Significant Side Effects**
   * Select the correct option for **“Did these side effects require medical attention?”**
5. Duty GP reviews the incident

If **non-urgent** side effect:

* Use the **Side Effect Macros** to contact them if needed
* **Do not** put the order on hold purely for non-urgent side effects
* You can usually still prescribe (assuming no contraindications)

---

### Step B — Do the 4 prescriber checks (this is the “core”)

The SOP’s detailed checks are basically:

#### 1) Weight loss checkpoint (licensing logic)

A patient should have lost **≥5%** of initial body weight by:

* **6 months** (Mounjaro/Wegovy)
* **4 months** (Nevolat)

If they **fail** this, your next steps depend on *how much* they lost:

##### ✅ If weight loss is **≥5%**

* Periodic review not mandatory (unless they chose to do it)
* Proceed normally

##### 🟠 If weight loss is **3–5%**

SOP allows *possible continuation* **if there’s a reasonable explanation**, e.g.:

* Evidence of muscle gain / waist reduction / increased resistance exercise
* Improvements in heart health
* Suboptimal compliance due to scheduling or manageable side effects
* Steady downward trajectory (not flat)
* Not reached maximum dose yet
* Gaps in treatment affecting progress
* Switched GLP-1 and hasn’t completed full 4/6 months on the new one
* Side effects limited titration

If you continue despite <5%:
✅ **Add a critical note** explaining why you continued + flag future prescribers to monitor weight loss closely.

##### 🔴 If weight loss is **<3%**

* SOP says **contact customer for a detailed assessment**
* Tag **Pending Customer Response**
* Use the info-gathering approach (diet, activity, adherence, gaps, side effects, new conditions/meds, etc.)

There’s even a call script in the SOP for **0–3% weight loss**, including:

* ID check (DOB + postcode)
* Confirm current height/weight
* Diet pattern and activity (with time categories)
* Adherence, gaps, side effects
* Any other reason for not losing weight
* Whether they’d consider an oral option like **orlistat** if GLP-1 can’t continue

##### ⛔ If overall weight is **unchanged or increased**

SOP is clear:

* If weight remains unchanged or has increased after **4/6 months**, tell them the medication is **no longer suitable** under licensing requirements and **cannot be continued** → cancel order.

---

#### 2) Side effects check

* Ensure side effects are appropriately handled
* Urgent = **phone + safety incident**
* Non-urgent = **side effect macros**, usually do not hold

---

#### 3) New conditions / new meds check

If they report anything new:

* Refer to the **GLP-1 Knowledge Base** (per SOP)
* If not a contraindication, document it
* If unsure → **escalate to senior prescriber**

---

#### 4) Compliance check (weekly injections)

They should be adhering to the schedule.

If non-compliance is due to:

* Side effects already dealt with → proceed to next check
* Scheduling issues but weight loss adequate → proceed
* Side effects *not captured earlier* → email to clarify + tag **Pending Customer Response**

---

## 5) Exemptions (so you don’t incorrectly block people)

### A) Transfers from other providers (exemption)

A transferred patient can be **exempt** from immediate periodic review if they meet **all 3**:

1. Start date on previous use form is **>7 months ago**
2. **No side effects** reported on previous use form
3. Latest weight is **>5% less** than starting weight on previous upload form

Also: breaks due to **pregnancy** are exempt.

If they don’t meet all 3 → invite to periodic review once they reach the 4/6-month mark (some may be asked earlier if side effects/poor weight loss).

### B) Gaps in treatment (manual exemption)

SOP says the system currently **doesn’t account for gaps**, so prescribers may need to **manually exempt**.

Manual exemption can apply if **all 3**:

1. Treatment gap **>12 weeks**
2. Restarted at the **lowest dose**
3. Since restarting, they’ve had **<3 months** treatment

If not all 3 → they complete periodic review at the standard 4/6-month mark from **first ever** treatment start date.

---

## 6) How you close it properly (people forget this)

Once satisfied:

1. Document in Notes: **“Complete In depth review at 4/6 months”**
2. Click **“Mark Resolved”**
3. Then proceed with reviewing their next order in line with **SOP-045**

---

## 7) The “cheat sheet” you should have in your head

When you open a periodic review, ask yourself in this order:

1. **Urgent side effect flag?**

   * Yes → urgent queue rules + phone within 8 working hours + safety incident
2. **Weight loss % at the mark?**

   * ≥5% → proceed
   * 3–5% → continue only if clear justification; add critical note
   * <3% → contact + pending response
   * unchanged/increased → stop + cancel
3. **Any new meds/conditions?**

   * If unsure → senior prescriber
4. **Adherence ok?**

   * If not, why? (side effects vs scheduling vs other)
